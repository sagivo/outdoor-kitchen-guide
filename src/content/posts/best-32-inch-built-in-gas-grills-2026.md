A 32-inch head is the sweet spot of the built-in gas grill world. It's big enough to cook for a family of four and handle a party — around 700–900 square inches of cooking area, roughly 18+ burgers at once — but it doesn't demand the 8-foot island and 42-inch budget of the commercial-class heads. Most buyers end up here, which is why the 32-inch class has the strongest lineup in the industry: Blaze, Napoleon, and Lion all lead with their 32-inch models.

This guide compares the three best 32-inch built-in gas grills for 2026 on real manufacturer specs — BTU, cooking area, burners, construction, warranty, and price — plus two 30-inch value alternatives worth knowing about. For the general buying framework (fuel type, sizing, what to look for), start with [how to choose a built-in grill](/posts/how-to-choose-built-in-grill/); for the brand-level rivalry, see [Napoleon vs Blaze vs Bull](/posts/napoleon-vs-blaze-vs-bull/).

## The three best 32-inch built-in gas grills

### Blaze Premium LTE 32 — best overall

The Blaze Premium LTE 32 is the grill that treats the island as its natural habitat. Four cast stainless burners at 14,000 BTU each give 60,000 BTU of main-burner firepower across 915 square inches of total cooking area (including the warming rack zone), and the whole body is 304 stainless — not 304 trim over 430 parts. Heat zone separators divide the cooking surface so you can run a screaming-hot sear zone beside a gentle veggie zone, and the double-lined hood holds heat while shrugging off weather.

The standout feature is the warranty: lifetime coverage on the grill body, which is the part that fails on lesser grills — rusted fireboxes and corroded cart frames are what kill outdoor grills, not dead burners. The tradeoff is honest: no infrared or rotisserie burner at this price, so searing is direct-heat only, and it's heavy — plan the island structure and bring help to set it. If your island is a 10-year commitment, [the Blaze Premium LTE 32](/grills/blaze-premium-lte-32/) is the rational pick.

### Napoleon Prestige 500 Built-In — best for rotisserie and searing

The Napoleon Prestige 500 Built-In is the pick for buyers who want every cooking technique in one head. Four main burners plus a rear infrared rotisserie burner bring the all-in total to 76,000 BTU, and the thick 9mm stainless cooking grids hold heat like cast iron without the seasoning ritual. An integrated trim ring is designed for a clean drop-in install that forgives minor cutout imperfections — a genuinely useful feature when your stonecutter is working to tolerances.

You pay for the badge: it's premium-priced for a 32-inch head, and the island cutout is sizable, so measure twice. But if rotisserie chickens, slow-roasted prime rib, and hard sears are on your menu, [the Napoleon Prestige 500](/grills/napoleon-prestige-500-built-in/) is the most versatile 32-inch head in this comparison.

### Lion Premium L75000 32 — most firepower per dollar

Lion's headline is raw output: 90,000 BTU all-in, the highest total rating in the 32-inch class, across 830 square inches with cast stainless burners and a rear infrared rotisserie burner included. Street prices around ~$2,499–$2,999 make it the hottest 32-inch head per dollar you spend.

Read the number honestly: the main grilling burners are a conventional 60,000 BTU, with the rear infrared burner accounting for the rest. That's still serious searing and rotisserie headroom — but two practical cautions. First, the brand's support network is smaller than Napoleon's or Blaze's, so buy from a dealer you trust. Second, the big BTU number needs a properly sized gas line; mention the total rating to your installer when they size the run. [The Lion L75000 32](/grills/lion-l75000-32/) is the right pick for buyers who want maximum grill for the money and have a good installer on the job.

## Worth knowing: two 30-inch value alternatives

These aren't 32-inch heads — cutouts differ and they won't interchange — but if your island budget is tight, they're legitimate downsize options:

- **[Bull Outlaw 30](/grills/bull-outlaw-30/)** (~$2,299–$2,699): four 15,000 BTU burners, 60,000 BTU total, 810 square inches, and a rear infrared rotisserie burner — solid 304 stainless at a lower price than Blaze or Napoleon, with fit and finish a half-step below the top tier.
- **[Coyote S-Series 30](/grills/coyote-s-series-30/)** (~$1,899–$2,299): three burners at 60,000 BTU total and 700 square inches, but with premium touches — interior grill lighting, LED-lit knobs, ceramic briquettes, full 304 stainless — at a mid-range price. Three burners limit multi-zone cooking, but it's the cheapest path to a stainless built-in from a reputable brand.

## What to check before you buy

**Warranty as a cost proxy.** The warranty terms track closely with build quality in this class. Blaze's lifetime body warranty reflects 304-stainless construction that resists corrosion; budget heads that carry 1–5 year warranties on the body are telling you where they saved money. Over a 10-year ownership period, the grill with the better warranty is often the cheaper grill — replacement fireboxes and hoods are not small purchases.

**Fuel type.** All three 32-inch heads come in separate propane and natural gas versions and are usually not field-convertible — decide before ordering. Natural gas is cheaper to run and never runs out mid-cook, but needs a gas line to the island; our [natural gas vs propane](/posts/natural-gas-vs-propane-grill/) guide works through the math.

**Cutout first.** 32-inch heads are not interchangeable between brands — Blaze's ~30¾" × 21¼" cutout won't match another brand's opening. Buy the grill, then cut the island. Our [installation clearances and safety guide](/posts/built-in-grill-installation-clearances-safety/) covers the full framing, ventilation, and gas rules.

**Total cost.** The grill head is typically a third to half of the finished island. Budget the countertop, framing, gas line, and any vent hood alongside the head — the [outdoor kitchen cost breakdown](/posts/outdoor-kitchen-cost-breakdown/) has real ranges. And plan the accessories now: a [side burner](/posts/best-side-burner-outdoor-kitchen/) belongs in the gas-line sizing from day one, not as a year-two retrofit.

## Bottom line

- **Best overall:** Blaze Premium LTE 32 — 304 stainless, zone separators, lifetime body warranty; the grill built for the hole in your countertop.
- **Best for rotisserie + searing:** Napoleon Prestige 500 Built-In — rear infrared burner and 9mm grids for maximum technique range.
- **Most firepower per dollar:** Lion L75000 32 — 90,000 BTU all-in for ~$2,499–$2,999, assuming you have a good installer.

Any of the three is a grill you'll hand down. Match it to how you cook — and to the island you're building around it.
