Outdoor pizza ovens went from enthusiast toy to mainstream in about five years, and the 2026 market is mature: gas ovens that hit 950°F for $500, wood-fired options for less, and centerpiece ovens that anchor an outdoor kitchen. Here are the five worth buying.

[[chart:temp]]

## Our top picks at a glance

**Best overall: [Ooni Koda 16](amazon:ooni-koda-16).** 16-inch deck, 950°F, propane convenience. The oven for pizza night with friends.

**Best 12-inch: [Gozney Roccbox](amazon:gozney-roccbox).** The rolling-flame baffle cooks the most even 12-inch pizza under $600, and it holds heat between bakes better than anything near its price.

**Best splurge: [Gozney Dome S1](amazon:gozney-dome-s1).** Professional heat management and precise gas control for the committed pizza cook.

**Best budget wood-fired: [Ooni Karu 12](amazon:ooni-karu-12).** Real wood fire from under $400 — with a learning curve attached.

**Cheapest entry: [Solo Stove Pi Prime](amazon:solo-stove-pi-prime).** 950°F for the lowest price in the category. The low-risk way to test pizza-oven life.

## Gas vs wood vs multi-fuel

This is the real decision, more than brand. **Gas** is consistent and effortless — the right call for weeknight pizza. **Wood/charcoal** adds smoky flavor you cannot fake, at the cost of tending a fire. **Multi-fuel** (Karu 12, or Roccbox with the wood attachment) starts wood and can add gas later. Our [fuel comparison tool](/tools/pizza-oven-fuel-compare/) puts numbers on preheat time and cost per pizza.

## Size: 12-inch vs 16-inch

A 12-inch oven serves 1–2 people per launch; a 16-inch oven serves 3–4. For parties, 16 inches halves your time at the oven. But 16-inch ovens are bigger, heavier, and hungrier — don't buy capacity you won't use.

## What else you need

Budget $100–$200 for the essentials: a perforated metal peel for launching, a turning peel, and an infrared thermometer. The thermometer matters more than any accessory — launching onto a stone that isn't hot enough is the #1 beginner mistake.

## The bottom line

Buy the Koda 16 for crowds, the Roccbox for the best small pizza, the Dome S1 if pizza is the lifestyle, the Karu 12 for wood flavor on a budget, or the Pi Prime to find out if you're an oven person. Then make dough — the oven is only half the equation.
