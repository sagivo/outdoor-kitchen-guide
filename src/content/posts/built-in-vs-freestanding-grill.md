The built-in vs freestanding decision usually makes itself once you answer one question: **are you building an outdoor kitchen island?** If yes, you want a built-in head. If no, a freestanding grill is the sane choice. But the trade-offs underneath that answer — cost, performance, flexibility, resale — deserve a real look, because plenty of buyers land in the middle.

## What each one actually is

A **built-in grill** is just the cooking head — burners, grates, hood, no cart — designed to drop into a cutout in a masonry or framed island. It's permanently installed, usually plumbed to natural gas (or a hidden propane setup), and surrounded by countertop, storage, and often a sink or fridge. Our [best built-in gas grills](/posts/best-built-in-gas-grills-2026/) roundup covers the top heads.

A **freestanding grill** is the familiar cart grill: head on a wheeled cart with side shelves, usually running on a 20 lb propane tank tucked underneath. Unbox, assemble in an hour or two, grill tonight.

## Cost: the real gap is the island, not the grill

A premium freestanding gas grill runs roughly $500–$2,500 all-in. A premium built-in head alone runs roughly $2,000–$3,500 — not wildly more. The gap explodes with the island: masonry or framed construction, countertops, gas and electrical runs, permits, and labor typically add $5,000–$15,000+ to the project. Our [outdoor kitchen cost breakdown](/posts/outdoor-kitchen-cost-breakdown/) itemizes every line, and the [build cost estimator](/tools/build-cost-estimator/) lets you model your version.

So the honest math: a built-in setup costs **3–5x** a freestanding grill, and 80%+ of that premium is the island, not the head. If the island is happening regardless, the head choice is easy. If the island is the thing you're debating, debate the island.

## Cooking performance: closer than the price suggests

Here's the uncomfortable truth for built-in fans: **a great freestanding grill outcooks a mediocre built-in head.** Burner design, grate quality, and heat distribution matter more than the mounting style. A $1,500 freestanding grill from a good brand will sear better than a $900 drop-in head of dubious origin.

Where built-ins pull ahead is at the top end: 60,000+ BTU across four burners, infrared rear burners and rotisseries, 9mm stainless grids, and double-lined hoods are standard on premium heads like the [Napoleon Prestige 500 Built-In](/grills/napoleon-prestige-500-built-in/) and [Blaze Premium LTE 32](/grills/blaze-premium-lte-32/) — features that are rare or absent on carts. If you want the absolute best cooking hardware, it's sold as a built-in head.

## Flexibility vs permanence

**Freestanding wins on flexibility.** Move it for a party. Roll it into the garage for winter. Take it when you move. Try charcoal next year without demolishing masonry. For renters, this isn't a debate — freestanding is the only option.

**Built-in wins on permanence.** It becomes part of the house: no wheels to wobble, no tank to look at, gas and electric neatly hidden, everything at counter height. And permanence has a financial dimension — a well-executed outdoor kitchen is a genuine selling feature, while a cart grill adds nothing to an appraisal.

The permanence cuts both ways, though. Built-in heads are brand-specific sizes — a 32-inch Napoleon cutout won't fit a 32-inch Blaze without modification. If you think you'll want a different head in five years, know that swaps can mean stonework.

## Space and layout

Freestanding grills need surprisingly little: a level surface and a few feet of clearance. They're the right answer for patios, decks, balconies (where gas is allowed), and renters.

Built-ins demand commitment: the island footprint (typically 6–10 feet long), utility trenching, and clearances from structures. They also demand planning around [ventilation](/posts/outdoor-kitchen-ventilation-guide/) if there's any overhead cover. Small space? Read our [small outdoor kitchen ideas](/posts/small-outdoor-kitchen-ideas/) before assuming you need the full island.

## Maintenance and longevity

**Built-in heads are built to be rebuilt.** Premium heads use 304 stainless burners, heavy grates, and simple mechanical ignitions with widely available replacement parts. A Blaze or Napoleon head can realistically serve 15–20+ years with occasional burner or igniter swaps — and the lifetime body warranty on some brands means the expensive part never needs replacing. The island itself (masonry, stone, quality countertops) is essentially permanent.

**Freestanding grills are consumables by comparison.** Wheels, cart frames, and thinner fireboxes live harder lives — they're moved, rained on, and knocked around. A good freestanding grill lasts 5–10 years; a cheap one lasts 3–5. Replacement is the maintenance plan, which is fine at cart prices but worth knowing going in.

The maintenance you actually do is similar for both: brush grates after every cook, deep-clean twice a season, check burners for clogs annually, and keep it covered. Built-ins just reward that care with decades instead of years.

## Resale value: what the numbers suggest

A permanent outdoor kitchen is one of the few backyard projects that consistently shows up on "features buyers pay for" lists, alongside pools (in warm climates) and covered patios. Real estate analyses regularly find quality outdoor kitchens returning a meaningful share of their cost at resale — estimates cluster around 50–70%+ for well-executed builds in markets where outdoor living matters, plus the harder-to-measure effect of selling faster.

A freestanding grill, however nice, contributes nothing to appraised value — it's personal property that leaves with you. So if you're weighing a $12,000 island against a $1,500 cart, part of the island's cost is an investment in the house, not just the cooking. That doesn't make the island "cheap," but it changes the comparison from pure expense to expense-plus-asset.

## The hybrid path most people ignore

You don't have to choose forever. A legitimate strategy: buy a quality freestanding grill now, grill for a season or two, and learn what you actually cook. Then build the island around a built-in head with real knowledge of your habits — including which [fuel type](/posts/natural-gas-vs-propane-grill/) you want plumbed in. You'll make a better $10,000 decision after 50 cooks than after zero.

## Who should buy which

**Buy built-in if:** you own the home, you're building (or already have) the island, you grill weekly or more, you want the best available hardware, and you care about the finished look and resale.

**Buy freestanding if:** you rent, your patio is small, your budget is under ~$2,500, you might move in a few years, or you just want to grill great food without a construction project.
