Three brands dominate the premium built-in conversation: Napoleon (features), Blaze (warranty), and Bull (value). All three make 304-stainless, 30–32 inch heads in the $2,300–$3,200 range. Here's how to choose between them.

## The short version

- **Buy the [Blaze Premium LTE 32](amazon:blaze-premium-lte-32)** if the grill must last 15+ years outdoors. Lifetime body warranty, heat zone separators, zero gimmicks.
- **Buy the [Napoleon Prestige 500 Built-In](amazon:napoleon-prestige-500-built-in)** if you want the most versatile cooker: infrared rear burner, rotisserie, thick grids.
- **Buy the [Bull Outlaw 30](amazon:bull-outlaw-30)** if you want 90% of the experience and would rather spend the savings on the countertop.

## Head-to-head

**Firepower:** Napoleon leads on paper (~76,000 BTU with the rear burner), but all three put 60,000 BTU through the main burners — the number that matters for grilling. Tie in practice.

**Cooking versatility:** Napoleon wins clearly. The infrared rear burner plus rotisserie kit opens up whole chickens, roasts, and ribs. Bull matches it with its own rear IR burner. Blaze has no rear burner — its versatility comes from zone separators instead.

**Materials:** All three use 304 stainless bodies. Blaze's double-lined hood and heavier gauge feel the most overbuilt; Napoleon's 9mm grids are the best cooking surface; Bull is solid but a half-step down in fit and finish.

**Warranty:** Blaze wins by a mile — lifetime on the body. Napoleon and Bull are limited (roughly 15 years on major parts, less on wear items). If you keep grills for decades, this decides it.

**Price:** Bull is typically $300–$600 less than the other two. Napoleon sits at the top of the range.

## Who should buy which

- **The forever grill:** Blaze. You are buying the last built-in head you will need.
- **The cook's grill:** Napoleon. You will use the rotisserie monthly.
- **The smart-money grill:** Bull. You want premium specs and a premium island, and the budget has to cover both.
