Fuel type is the most consequential pizza oven decision — more than brand, more than size. It determines how you cook, not just what you cook.

## Gas: the default

**How it works:** Propane burner, dial control, 950°F in 15–25 minutes.
**Pros:** Push-button convenience, consistent heat, fast recovery between bakes, cheap to run.
**Cons:** No wood-smoke flavor; needs propane refills.
**Best for:** Weeknight pizza, beginners, anyone who values consistency. Ovens: [Ooni Koda 16](amazon:ooni-koda-16), [Gozney Roccbox](amazon:gozney-roccbox), [Solo Stove Pi Prime](amazon:solo-stove-pi-prime).

## Wood/charcoal: the flavor pick

**How it works:** You build and tend a small fire; the oven hits 950°F on radiant heat.
**Pros:** Real wood-fired flavor, romantic process, no propane logistics.
**Cons:** Genuine skill curve — expect scorched crusts while learning; ash cleanup; you must stay with the fire.
**Best for:** Enthusiasts who enjoy fire craft. Ovens: [Ooni Karu 12](amazon:ooni-karu-12) (multi-fuel).

## Multi-fuel: the hedge

Ovens like the Karu 12 burn wood/charcoal out of the box and accept an optional gas burner. Start with wood romance; add gas when a Tuesday night demands pizza in 20 minutes. The burner costs extra, so price the full package before comparing against a gas-only oven.

## Electric: the newcomer

Electric pizza ovens (like Solo Stove's electric Pi and various indoor/outdoor models) trade max temperature for convenience — most top out around 750–850°F rather than 950°F, which means excellent New York style but not true Neapolitan. The killer feature: no flame, so they work where gas and wood are banned, including indoors.

## Running costs compared

Use our [fuel comparison tool](/tools/pizza-oven-fuel-compare/) for your exact numbers, but the shape is: propane costs a few dollars per pizza night; hardwood splits cost more per session but you use less of them; electricity is cheapest per bake but the oven costs more up front.

## Which fuel for your setup?

**Small patio or balcony:** gas (or electric where flame is banned). Wood needs firewood storage and produces more smoke — bad neighbors in tight quarters. See our [small outdoor kitchen ideas](/posts/small-outdoor-kitchen-ideas/) for compact-friendly setups.

**Full outdoor kitchen island:** gas plumbed alongside the grill's gas line is the cleanest install — one line, two appliances. A [multi-fuel oven](/grills/ooni-karu-12/) adds wood-fired weekends without a second gas drop.

**The pizza obsessive:** wood or multi-fuel. You'll develop the fire skills, and the flavor payoff on long bakes is real.

**Renters:** portable gas ovens like the [Solo Stove Pi Prime](/grills/solo-stove-pi-prime/) — no installation, moves with you.
