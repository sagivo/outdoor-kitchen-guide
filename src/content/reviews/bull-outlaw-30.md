The Bull Outlaw 30 is the value pick of the premium built-ins: four 15,000 BTU burners, 810 square inches of cooking space, and a rear infrared rotisserie burner, all for less than a Blaze or Napoleon of the same size.

## Who the Outlaw 30 is for (and who should skip it)

It fits the builder whose budget has to cover the whole project — island, countertop, gas line — not just the head. Saving $400–$600 here versus a Blaze buys a meaningful countertop upgrade.

Skip it if you want the most refined experience: knob feel, lid action, and interior lighting are where the premium brands justify their prices. And skip it if you need maximum warranty — Bull's coverage is solid but not lifetime.

## Cooking performance

Sixty thousand BTU across four burners is the sweet spot for a 30-inch head: enough for hard searing with all zones lit, controllable enough for low-and-slow with one burner on. The rear infrared burner handles rotisserie duty well — whole chickens come out evenly browned without the flare-ups of direct-heat rotisserie.

## Build and installation notes

The dual-lined hood is a real asset in wind, and the 304 stainless body handles weather without babying. Cutout dimensions are standard for the class, but as with every built-in, confirm the manual's numbers before the contractor cuts. Leave service clearance behind the island for the gas connection.

## How it compares

Against the [Blaze Premium LTE 32](/grills/blaze-premium-lte-32/), you're trading lifetime warranty and zone separators for a rear infrared burner and several hundred dollars back. Against the [Napoleon Prestige 500](/grills/napoleon-prestige-500-built-in/), you're trading thicker grids and finer fit-and-finish for a lower price on nearly identical cooking capability.
