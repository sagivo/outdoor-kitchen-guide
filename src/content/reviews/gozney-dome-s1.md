The Gozney Dome S1 is the centerpiece oven: Gozney's professional heat management in a gas-only package that costs meaningfully less than the dual-fuel Dome flagship. If the pizza oven is the reason the outdoor kitchen exists, this is the oven.

## Who the Dome S1 is for (and who should skip it)

It fits the dedicated pizza cook — someone making dough weekly, hosting pizza nights, and willing to pay for the last 10% of performance. The 16-inch deck and precise gas control reward skill.

Skip it if you are pizza-curious rather than pizza-committed: a Koda 16 cooks 90% as well for a third of the price. And skip it if you want wood — the S1 is gas only by design.

## Heat management

This is where the money goes. The rolling flame and stone mass hold temperature through long sessions without the sag that plagues thin portable ovens, and the gas dial gives you real control — run 950°F for Neapolitan, back it down for New York style or roasting. Few ovens under $2,000 offer this range.

## Size and permanence

At 58 pounds the Dome S1 is not moving once you place it — plan its home like an appliance, ideally integrated into the outdoor kitchen counter run. It earns the footprint.

## The Dome S1 in the outdoor kitchen

At 58 pounds plus its footprint, the Dome S1 wants a permanent home — ideally its own counter section in the island, near the grill but with clear landing space on both sides for peels. If your kitchen is under a roof, check the [ventilation guide](/posts/outdoor-kitchen-ventilation-guide/): pizza ovens produce serious heat and smoke, and a covered setup needs a plan. For the gas-vs-wood decision, our [fuel guide](/posts/pizza-oven-fuel-guide/) covers why most owners end up on gas 80% of the time.

The best gas pizza oven most people can buy, at a price that demands commitment. If pizza night is a lifestyle, it is worth it. Compare it against the [Ooni Koda 16](/grills/ooni-koda-16/) in [Ooni vs Gozney](/posts/ooni-vs-gozney/).
