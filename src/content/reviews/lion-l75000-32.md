The Lion L75000 is the firepower pick: a 90,000 BTU all-in rating makes it the hottest 32-inch built-in head in this guide, with cast stainless burners and a rear infrared rotisserie burner included.

## Who the L75000 is for (and who should skip it)

It fits the steak-first griller who wants maximum searing headroom per dollar. If your idea of a good Saturday is a thick ribeye with a hard crust, the extra BTU buys real results.

Skip it if your gas line is undersized or your installer is iffy — this grill punishes weak infrastructure. And skip it if brand support matters more to you than output; Blaze and Napoleon have deeper service networks.

## What the BTU number really means

Treat the 90,000 BTU as searing and rotisserie headroom, not everyday cooking power. Day to day you will grill on the four main burners at 60,000 BTU, which is exactly right for 830 square inches. The rear infrared burner is where the big number shows: rotisserie chickens brown faster and more evenly than on direct-heat spits.

## Who actually needs this much heat?

Be honest: most weeknight grilling never touches 90,000 BTU. The Lion earns its firepower in three scenarios — hard searing thick steaks, rotisserie for large cuts, and high-heat cooking for a crowd where recovery time matters. If that's your cooking, the Lion is the value play of the premium tier. If you mostly do burgers and chicken, a [Bull Outlaw 30](/grills/bull-outlaw-30/) cooks identically for less money and sips gas doing it. And whichever head you choose, settle the [natural gas vs propane](/posts/natural-gas-vs-propane-grill/) question before the installer arrives — the Lion is thirsty enough that fuel costs matter.

## The bottom line

The most grill per dollar here, with one condition: confirm your gas supply can feed it. Do that, and it is hard to beat.
